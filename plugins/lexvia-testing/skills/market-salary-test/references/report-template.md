# Salary report structure

Use this structure with actual researched content. Do not leave placeholders in the completed report. A sparse-evidence result must say what could and could not be established rather than fill the fields with guesses.

## Recommendation

State the job title, assessment date, geography, recommended annual base salary range, recommended target salary, AUD/before-tax basis, exclusion of employer super, and confidence with its reason. Cite the evidence IDs and calculated-summary ID supporting the range and target. If a range cannot be established, make this explicit in the recommendation.

Explain in one short paragraph what the target role does and why the suggested pay is supported. Label the result as an evidence-based benchmarking recommendation, rather than a guaranteed offer or statistically representative market estimate.

## Scope and assumptions

| Item | Basis used |
| --- | --- |
| Role interpretation and seniority | State the likely duties and level, distinguishing user input from inference. |
| Location | Australia-wide unless specified; identify geographical gaps. |
| Employment and hours | Permanent full-time by default; identify any assumed hours used in a calculation. |
| Included pay | Gross annual base salary in AUD. |
| Separately reported pay | Employer superannuation, bonus, commission, allowances, overtime and benefits. |
| Research coverage | Search date/window and actual source data periods. |

## Evidence register

Use two tables if one would become too wide. Retain one stable source ID across both.

| ID | Publisher / job or benchmark | Location and level | Original salary and unit | Pay basis / hours | Data period | Relevance and use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

| ID | Page title and direct public URL | Publication or ad date | Access date | Normalized annual base amount | Conversion or exclusion reason |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

Explicitly distinguish direct advertisements, surveys, occupation statistics and legal minima. Include relevant excluded/context-only sources so readers can see why a quoted figure was not used. Do not reproduce lengthy copyrighted excerpts from advertisements or guides.

## Calculations and selection

Give each derived result a calculation ID such as C1. Show original inputs with source IDs, formula, sample size, result, and any analytical assumptions. Make it possible to reproduce the recommended endpoints and target from the evidence register. Describe rounding separately.

Explain which sources formed the primary cohort, how the guide/government evidence cross-checks it, and why conflicting figures or outliers were excluded or separated. Label sample medians and annualized figures accurately; do not present advertised salaries as actual accepted pay.

## How to use the range

Explain the duties, experience or responsibility differences that support the lower end, target and upper end, without inventing a fixed percentage adjustment. If the title requires distinct occupations or locations, show separately supported scenarios with their own evidence IDs instead of one blended range.

## Minimum-pay check and limitations

State the award/agreement and classification only if verified; otherwise explain what information is missing. Cite any minimum rate and its effective date. Keep the minimum-pay result separate from market statistics. Do not claim a compliance pass based only on a job title.

Explain limitations that materially affect this result: data age, few employers, uncertain packages, broad occupational groups, missing locations, unclear role scope, or unavailable live research. For migration requests, add the conditional checks from `migration-check.md` and state any employer evidence still needed.
